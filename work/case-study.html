02 The challenge

When a team is this busy, nothing gets ignored on purpose. It drops out of sight, then out of mind, until it's late.

Client requests, questions to answer, items to order, changes to the schedule. Across every active home, keeping up with all of it was daunting, and things got missed or done late.

03 What they asked for

No new software.

The brief was to build the to-do lists for the whole team, without asking anyone to adopt a new tool. So nobody logs in and nobody learns anything. Their day looks the way it did before. The tasks just show up.

Works as it is

In the inbox.

Each person's tasks arrive by email, in the inbox they already check several times a day. Nothing to install. Nothing to learn.

Optional upgrade

On a Trello board.

Teams who want a board per person can have one. The system runs 100% without it, so nobody gets pushed onto it.

04 How it decides

It reads like a person. It routes like your team.

01

Reads every comment.

Every comment is read in context. The rest of the thread stays attached, so even a one-line reply makes sense.

02

Works out who it's for.

Not just whoever got @mentioned. Plenty of mentions are only a cc, and the task belongs to someone else. So it follows the team's own rules for what goes to whom, and treats a mention as one clue, not the answer.

03

Puts it where they'll look.

One task per person, in the inbox they already check, plus a daily overview. Every decision is logged, and a human review point keeps a person in charge.

One rule, on purpose

It never contacts a client. Anything client-facing stays with a person.

Before it touched a live email, we tested it hard on our side and set targets in advance: 95% routing accuracy, 99% capture, and every comment read. Then it ran for a full week alongside the team's normal workflow, not instead of it. It read every comment and sent every reviewed task to the right person, so two of the three targets were met. Capture came in at 34 of 35 tasks. That is 97.1% against a 99% target, and on a sample this small a single missed task moves the number almost three points. The miss was a "ready for the next step" handoff between colleagues. We added a rule for that pattern after the test.

08 Beyond week one

What's next.

In the first week, work reached the right people without anyone changing how they work. Here is what we have left open on purpose.

  • →

    Closing the loop.

    NowEvery card stays open until a person closes it.

    NextIt remembers its open cards and moves one to Done when a later comment answers it. A short "done in the last 24 hours" list catches any wrong close.

  • →

    Faster than a day.

    NowTasks land with the morning summary, so each day starts with a ready list.

    NextReading messages as they arrive would cut the wait. It follows closing the loop, so a question doesn't become a task before it's answered.

  • →

    People outside the team.

    NowEvery task goes to a team member, who handles any vendor or contractor side.

    NextIt could email vendors and contractors directly. The project software can't post a reply, so a person would log those messages.

  • →

    Measuring time saved.
